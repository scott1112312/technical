1.  Executive Summary
1.1  Matters requiring attention
Three facts established during this review differ from the assumptions applied in the working session. Each has a direct bearing on transaction perimeter, valuation or structuring, and should be confirmed with the deal team before materials are drafted.
Finding 1 — PKU Healthcare Corp. (000788.SZ) is no longer within the Ping An perimeter
In December 2024, Ping An disposed of its controlling interest in the Shenzhen-listed pharmaceutical company for a consideration of RMB 1 for the equity, plus approximately RMB 33 million for related shareholder debt. Control passed to Xinyoushi International (Hangzhou), with Mr. Xu Xiren becoming the ultimate controlling party. The listed company has since been required to remove all "PKU" branding.
PKU Healthcare Management retains only a residual financial interest of approximately 10.7%, which is pledged and has been progressively sold down since October 2025.
Implication: the 34.02% aggregate holding referenced in discussion is a pre-December 2024 figure. The listed pharmaceutical business should not be valued as part of the injection perimeter, and the A-share regulatory considerations previously identified are largely no longer applicable.

Finding 2 — Peking University International Hospital is a not-for-profit medical institution
Under PRC law, a not-for-profit medical institution may not distribute profits to its sponsors, and sponsors have no right to sell the institution; surplus must be applied to the institution itself. Such an entity cannot be injected into a listed company by way of a conventional equity transfer.
Two established routes exist, both of which carry material execution risk: (i) conversion to for-profit status, which requires deregistration and re-establishment and, for a Grade IIIA hospital, provincial-level approval; or (ii) a management or entrustment arrangement under which economic benefit is extracted through management and consulting fees, with consolidation achieved on a control basis.
Implication: this is a Section 3 execution item and, in our view, one of the more credible candidates for the "innovative structure" the client has indicated it is looking for.

Finding 3 — PKU Healthcare Information Technology sits outside the Group
The medical informatics business reports to Founder Information Industry Group rather than to PKU Healthcare Management, and has been renamed Beijing Yixin Shuzhi Technology. The Group's own website identifies it as a cooperation partner rather than a subsidiary. It should be excluded from the perimeter.

3.3  Observations
•Multiple state-owned minority shareholders. Beyond Huafa at the holding-company level, minorities sit at asset level: Shanxi Lu'an Mining (40% of Lu'an Hospital), CNOOC Bohai (40% of CNOOC Hospital) and Shandong Aluminium (28.97% of Zibo Hospital). If the injection requires movement at hospital level, pre-emption or consent rights may be engaged, and each of these counterparties carries its own state-asset valuation and approval process.
•The rehabilitation platform is not controlled. PKU Care Industrial Group Holdings holds only 33.125% of PKU Care Rehabilitation Hospital Management, alongside two 31.25% holders including an offshore private equity vehicle. Any disposal of this asset requires third-party agreement.
•Registered capital is not informative for valuation. Peking University International Hospital carries registered capital of only RMB 150 million against cumulative construction investment of RMB 4.5 billion. Registered capital should not be used as a valuation reference point.
•Multi-layered holdings. Several assets are held across two platforms simultaneously — Lu'an Hospital, for example, is held 30% by Industrial Group Holdings and 30% by Beiyi Hospital Management. Effective look-through interests should be calculated rather than read off any single register.

6.  Transaction Considerations
Ordered by significance. The first two items were not addressed in the working session.
6.1  Not-for-profit status of the flagship hospital
A not-for-profit medical institution does not distribute profit to its sponsors and its sponsors have no right to sell it. It cannot therefore be transferred into a listed company by conventional means. Two routes are observed in the market.
Route	Mechanism	Principal difficulties
Conversion to for-profit	Internal resolution, financial audit, competent authority approval, establishment of a new company capitalised with the institution's net assets and assuming its receivables and payables, followed by deregistration of the original entity.	No unified national implementing rules; only certain provinces have published a path. Involves real property transfer and conversion of allocated land to granted land, together with transfer of medical personnel. Practitioner consensus is that conversion of a Grade IIIA hospital requires provincial-level approval and is very difficult.
Management / entrustment	Status is unchanged; the listed company consolidates on a control basis, with economic benefit extracted through management and consulting fees.	Hong Kong precedent exists. The determining questions are whether consolidation is supportable and whether the auditor will accept it.
A layered structure is worth considering — for-profit subsidiaries (clinics, health management, rehabilitation, the industrial park) transferred by way of equity, with the not-for-profit institutions addressed through a management arrangement. This is the kind of structuring answer the client has indicated it is looking for, and represents a point of differentiation.
Point to verify
The for-profit or not-for-profit status of Luzhong, Zibo, Lu'an and CNOOC Hospitals is not established in public sources. This determines what proportion of the asset base is affected, and is a specific, low-sensitivity question that is more likely to obtain a response than a general request for financial information.
6.2  Peking University brand licensing
In connection with the December 2024 disposal of the listed pharmaceutical business, Peking University required that the company and its subsidiaries cease to use "PKU", "PKU Healthcare", "Beiyi" or similar designations. The renaming programme was initiated in August 2025.
The implication is that the "PKU" brand is not an owned asset of the Group. It is a licence granted by Peking University under the 2013 strategic cooperation agreement and is capable of being withdrawn.
This matters for valuation. The Group's brand premium, and the academic and clinical support of the PKU Health Science Centre — approximately two thirds of the flagship hospital's physicians are drawn from other PKU-affiliated hospitals — rest on that relationship. Whether Peking University will continue to license the brand following a change in the control chain, and on what terms, is a diligence question that must be raised. The precedent of the listed pharmaceutical business indicates that the university does exercise these rights.
In our view this consideration bears on transaction feasibility at least as directly as valuation does, and warrants its own treatment in Section 3.

Ping An Life — filing of non-insurance subsidiary information, July 2026	Complete corporate tree, registered and paid-in capital, legal representatives and shareholding percentages. This is the most significant source identified in this review. Ping An Life is required to publish particulars of all non-insurance subsidiaries periodically, and it is the only public source from which a complete list of the Group's subsidiaries and interests can be obtained. Recommend saving to the deal folder and monitoring for updated versions.
PKU Healthcare Corp. (000788.SZ) — announcements and annual reports (cninfo)	Change of control, related-party transaction amounts, disposal announcements, New Founder ownership. The value lies in the related-party disclosure as a means of inferring hospital procurement scale, rather than in group-level performance.
Ping An Healthcare and Technology (1833.HK) — FY2024 and FY2025 results announcements	Revenue, profit, dividend and offer particulars. FY2025 released 24 March 2026.
